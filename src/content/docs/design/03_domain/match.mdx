## Purpose

A Match records that a specific Game was played, by whom, under what shared
configuration, and with what result for each participant. It is the anchor
of gaming history: everything else in this domain — identity resolution
and statistics — ultimately traces back to Matches.

A Match owns its MatchParticipants and optionally references a reusable
Playset: what was played besides the Game (expansions, variants, scenario).
Without a Playset it is the base game. It never references a
GhostPlayer or CanonicalPlayer directly — only through a MatchParticipant (see
**MatchParticipant**).

---

## Responsibilities

A Match is responsible for:

- referencing exactly one Game;
- referencing at most one Playset, shared across all its MatchParticipants;
  the expansions that were played are those of the Playset;
- optionally referencing which Community, if any, it was played within.
- owning one or more MatchParticipants, one per player;
- enforcing that no GhostPlayer appears in more than one MatchParticipant
within the same Match;
- recording its owner — the User who recorded it (the recorder);
- exposing its status: Draft or Finalized;
- exposing and enforcing its Edit Requests Toggle.

---

## Not Responsible For

A Match is NOT responsible for:

- identity resolution or claiming — that belongs to ClaimRequest and
ClaimEdge;
- computing statistics — Matches are read by statistics computation, not
responsible for it;
- deciding whether a proposed change is approved — that belongs to
MatchEdit; Match only enforces its own invariants once a change is
applied.

---

## Invariants

- A Match has exactly one owner, fixed at creation. Ownership is never
transferred.
- A Match has at least one MatchParticipant.
- A Match has at most one MatchParticipant per GhostPlayer.
- A Match has at most one Playset.
- A Match's Playset, if any, must belong to the same Game as the Match itself.
- A Match's Edit Requests Toggle can only be changed by its owner.
- A Draft Match is changed directly by its owner and accepts no MatchEdits.
- A Finalized Match never returns to Draft. Its participants, results,
Loadouts and Playset change only through a MatchEdit (see **MatchEdit**);
only when it was played and its Edit Requests Toggle remain directly
editable by its owner.
- A Finalized Match is never deleted.

---

## Lifecycle

### Draft

A Match is recorded by its owner as a Draft, together with its
MatchParticipants (and their Loadouts, where applicable) and, optionally, a
Playset and/or a Community reference. The Playset is an existing one, or a
new one created together with the Match.

While it is a Draft, the owner changes everything directly — the
MatchParticipants are replaced as a whole, and one that is left out is
removed — and may delete the Match, which removes it from everyone's
history.

### Finalized

The owner finalizes the Match once it is complete. Finalizing is one-way:
from then on the Match is part of every participant's gaming history and is
never deleted. Its MatchParticipants, results, Loadouts and which Playset it
references change only through a MatchEdit — which changes the reference,
never the content of any Playset, since Playset content is immutable (see
**Playset**). When it was played and its Edit Requests Toggle can still be
changed by its owner directly.

---

## Relationships

- Match references Game;
- Match references Playset (0..1);
- Match owns MatchParticipant (1..N);
- Match optionally references Community;
- Match is recorded by (owned by) User;
- Match is targeted by MatchEdit.

---

## Domain Events

- MatchRecorded
- MatchFinalized
- MatchDeleted (Draft only)
- EditRequestsEnabled
- EditRequestsDisabled

---

## Aggregate Design

Match is the Aggregate Root. MatchParticipant and Loadout are child
entities within it — none of them has identity or lifecycle independent of
their Match. All consistency rules that span multiple MatchParticipants (e.g.
no duplicate GhostPlayer, a coherent set of results) are enforced here,
which is also why MatchParticipant and Loadout were deliberately not modeled
as their own Aggregate Roots.
