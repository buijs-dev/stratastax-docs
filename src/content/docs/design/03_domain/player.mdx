## Purpose

"Player" is a conceptual grouping for the two ways a real-world person is
represented within Stratastax: as a **CanonicalPlayer** (a persistent,
authoritative identity) or as a **GhostPlayer** (a personal, reusable
reference kept by whoever recorded a match). Player is not itself a
persisted aggregate — see **Aggregate Design** below.

---

## Identity Model

### CanonicalPlayer

Represents the authoritative identity of a real person within Stratastax.
It is the stable identity used for:

- ownership of gaming history;
- statistics and achievements aggregation;
- Community participation across time.

A CanonicalPlayer is created together with a User during registration
(see **User**), in a fixed 1:1 relationship.

A CanonicalPlayer may be linked to multiple GhostPlayers, via ClaimEdges.

---

### GhostPlayer

A GhostPlayer is not a snapshot of a player. It is a **personal reference**,
owned by the User who created it (the recorder) — comparable to an entry in
that recorder's own address book. It exists to hold participation until the
real person joins Stratastax and it is claimed.

Key properties:

- owned by exactly one User (the recorder);
- reusable across any number of matches recorded by that owner;
- its display name is mutable by its owner (correcting a typo does not
change historical results);
- not globally unique — two different recorders can each have their own
GhostPlayer referring to the same or different real people, entirely
independently of one another;
- may remain unclaimed indefinitely — claiming is never required.

---

### Identity Relationship

GhostPlayers and CanonicalPlayers are connected through **claims**, not
transformation. A CanonicalPlayer does not replace a GhostPlayer. Instead,
it can be linked to one or more GhostPlayers:

```
CanonicalPlayer
    └── claims (via ClaimEdge) ──► GhostPlayer(s)
```

This relationship is:

- reversible;
- auditable;
- non-destructive to historical data.

---

## Responsibilities

CanonicalPlayer is responsible for:

- providing a stable identity for statistics, ratings, and achievements;
- participating in Communities over time;
- accumulating linked GhostPlayers via ClaimEdges.

GhostPlayer is responsible for:

- providing a stable, per-recorder reference usable across matches;
- allowing its display name to be corrected by its owner;
- serving as the anchor a Participation points to.

---

## Not Responsible For

Neither CanonicalPlayer nor GhostPlayer is responsible for:

- authentication;
- login credentials;
- notifications;
- preferences;
- privacy configuration.

These responsibilities belong to **User**.

Neither is responsible for:

- holding match-specific results (score, rank, outcome) — that belongs to
**Participation**;
- holding per-match, per-player configuration (faction, character, cards) —
that belongs to **Loadout**.

---

## Invariants

- A Match never references a CanonicalPlayer directly. It always references
a GhostPlayer, through a Participation.
- A GhostPlayer is owned by exactly one User for its entire lifetime.
- A GhostPlayer's identity (its owner, its creation context) is immutable;
its display name is not.
- Participation results do not change except through an approved MatchEdit,
regardless of any later change to the GhostPlayer's display name.
- A GhostPlayer can be referenced by any number of Participations belonging
to its owner.
- A GhostPlayer may remain unclaimed indefinitely.
- A GhostPlayer can be referenced by multiple ClaimRequests (conflicting
claims are allowed; only one ClaimEdge can be active at a time).
- A CanonicalPlayer can claim multiple GhostPlayers.
- Identity resolution never alters Match or Participation history.

---

## Lifecycle

### Creation

- GhostPlayer is created the first time its owner records a match with a
participant who does not yet have a linkable CanonicalPlayer.
- CanonicalPlayer is created during account registration, together with a
User.

### Evolution

- GhostPlayers persist and are reused across matches by the same owner;
their display name may be edited.
- CanonicalPlayers accumulate linked GhostPlayers over time, through
ClaimEdges created and resolved by Identity Resolution (see
**ClaimRequest**).

### End State

Neither a CanonicalPlayer nor a GhostPlayer is ever deleted while referenced
by gaming history.

---

## Relationships

- GhostPlayer is referenced by Participation;
- GhostPlayer is owned by User;
- CanonicalPlayer is claimed to GhostPlayer via ClaimEdge;
- CanonicalPlayer is targeted by ClaimRequests;
- CanonicalPlayer is linked to Statistics, Statistic Snapshots, and
Achievements;
- CanonicalPlayer participates in Communities;
- CanonicalPlayer is connected to exactly one User.

## Aggregate Design

**CanonicalPlayer** and **GhostPlayer** are each their own Aggregate Root.
"Player" itself is a sealed conceptual supertype at the language level
(e.g. `sealed interface Player`), not a separately persisted aggregate — it
has no consistency rules of its own beyond what its two members enforce
independently.

### Reasoning

CanonicalPlayer and GhostPlayer have different lifecycles, different
creation triggers, and different invariants (mutable display name vs.
persistent statistics anchor). Treating them as one aggregate would force
unrelated consistency boundaries together. Each enforces its own rules:

- GhostPlayer: ownership, reusability, display name mutability.
- CanonicalPlayer: claim relationships, historical integrity as seen from
the claiming side.

---

## Key Insight

Player identity is not a single object. It is two independent aggregates —
a persistent CanonicalPlayer and any number of personal, recorder-owned
GhostPlayers — connected through reversible ClaimEdges. A Match never needs
to know which of the two it is dealing with; it always points to a
GhostPlayer, through a Participation.
